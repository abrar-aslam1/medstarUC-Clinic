Cough, Fever or Sore Throat?
Where to Go This Flu Season in Murphy and Little Elm

By The MedStar care team · November 2026

By November, the calls to our front desk are mostly about coughs, fevers and sore throats, and the question behind most of them is the same: Do I need to be seen, and if so, where? Here is how we think about it, for families in Murphy, Little Elm and nearby.

Respiratory season is here

Flu, COVID-19, RSV and the common cold all circulate more in the cooler months. The Texas Department of State Health Services notes that flu season in the United States typically begins in October and often peaks from December through February. November is the start of the climb, which makes it a good month to plan ahead.

Most colds get better with rest and fluids. Some illnesses need a clinician, and a few need emergency care right away.

When to call 911 or go to the emergency room

If you think you are having a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. Do not wait to be seen at urgent care.

The CDC lists emergency warning signs of flu that mean you should get medical care right away. For adults, these include:

  • Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
  • Persistent pain or pressure in the chest or abdomen
  • Persistent dizziness, confusion, inability to arouse
  • Seizures
  • Not urinating
  • Severe muscle pain
  • Severe weakness or unsteadiness
  • Fever or cough that improve but then return or worsen
  • Worsening of chronic medical conditions

The CDC page also has a separate list for children. If you see any of these signs, go to the ER, not to us.

When walk-in urgent care is the right fit

Urgent care is for illnesses and injuries that are not life-threatening but should not wait for a routine appointment. During respiratory season, that often looks like:

  • A cough that isn't going away
  • A sore throat, especially with fever
  • Flu-like symptoms: fever, body aches, chills, fatigue
  • Allergy flare-ups or asthma symptoms that need attention (if you are struggling to breathe, that is an emergency)
  • A headache that comes along with a cold or flu-like illness

Our urgent care does not need an appointment. You can walk in during open hours, or call ahead at (972) 442-4700.

Primary care in Murphy and Little Elm: the doctor who knows your history

Urgent care handles today's problem. Primary care helps you stay ahead of the next one. Both of our clinics offer primary care with our board-certified family physicians, the same doctors who work in our urgent care.

A primary care visit is the right fit for:

  • Establishing care. New to Murphy or Little Elm, or without a regular doctor? A first visit gives us your history before you're sick.
  • Ongoing conditions. Diabetes, high blood pressure and high cholesterol all need steady attention through the winter. Regular visits let us adjust your plan before a cold or flu sets you back.
  • Annual check-ups. A yearly physical to review your health and medications.
  • Follow-ups after a sick visit. If you walk in with a bad cough on a Saturday at our Murphy clinic, you can follow up with the same practice, and often the same physician, during the week.
  • Your vaccine questions. The CDC advises people to stay up to date with recommended immunizations and to talk to their doctor or healthcare provider about what is recommended for them. A primary care visit is the right place for that conversation.

Our team speaks English, Urdu, Hindi, Punjabi and Arabic.

Looking for a primary care doctor in Murphy or Little Elm? Call (972) 442-4700 to book a visit at whichever clinic is closer to you. Primary care visits are scheduled, and we fit in same-day visits when we can.

A few things that help when you're sick

These are general habits the CDC recommends for preventing the spread of respiratory illness:

  • Wash your hands regularly and clean commonly touched surfaces.
  • Stay home and away from others while you are sick.
  • The CDC says you can go back to your normal activities when, for at least 24 hours, your symptoms are getting better overall and you have not had a fever without using fever-reducing medicine. It recommends extra precautions for the next five days.

When you come in, bring a photo ID, your insurance card and a list of your current medications. It also helps to note when your symptoms started.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an appointment to see a doctor in Murphy or Little Elm?

Not for urgent care. Walk-ins are welcome at both clinics during open hours. Primary care visits are scheduled. Call (972) 442-4700 to book at either clinic, and we fit in same-day visits when we can.

Should I go to urgent care or the ER for flu symptoms?

Urgent care can see you for flu-like symptoms such as fever, cough and body aches. If you have trouble breathing, chest pain or pressure, confusion, a seizure, or other emergency warning signs, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

Is your Little Elm clinic open on weekends?

No. Little Elm is open Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and Tuesday from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. Our Murphy clinic is open Saturday from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. and is closed on Sunday.

This post is general information, not medical advice for your situation. If you have a medical emergency, call 911.
